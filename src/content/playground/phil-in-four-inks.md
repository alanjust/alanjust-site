This is my friend Phil. I made his portrait the way a printing press makes color: four sheets of squares, one each for cyan, magenta, yellow and black, each tilted to its own angle (105°, 90°, 75° and 45°) so the grids don't fight each other. Stack them and a face appears. Pull them apart and each sheet on its own is barely a face at all.

There are three ways in, top to bottom:

- **The plates, pulled apart.** Turn the stack in 3D and spread the sheets apart. Switch from ink on paper to light in the dark and watch the same four sheets build Phil by taking light away, then by adding it.
- **Out of register.** Be the bad press. Every plate rides its own spring, black the stiffest and yellow the loosest, so a thumb on the sheet or a hard shake peels color fringes off his edges, and they wobble back into line.
- **Hand-colored.** He starts out in black and white. Drag through him to bring the color back, or pick a hue off the rainbow bar and repaint him a square at a time. What you paint stays until you reset.

It all works with a mouse, a finger or an Apple Pencil.

What I'd like to hear: which of the three makes the idea of four inks click for you, and which one you just wanted to keep playing with.
