<p><strong>What does an object actually show, before anyone tells you what it is?</strong></p><p>Museums catalog archaeological objects starting from the label: who made it, where, when. When the paperwork is thin, the label can get ahead of the evidence. The Grammar of Things looks first. It reads a photograph of the object with the catalog claim held back, scores what it sees against 29 visual principles, and only then compares that with what the record says.</p><p>Every sentence it writes is tagged by how it knows: direct observation, interpretation or scholarly consensus. You can see which parts are looking and which parts are guessing.</p><p>Built for Southwest collections and the IMLS National Leadership Grants for Museums. My own project, designed and built from scratch.</p><p><a href="https://thegrammarofthings.com" class="cta-link">Visit thegrammarofthings.com →</a></p>
